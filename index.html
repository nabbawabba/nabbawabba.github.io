About

A technical focus with room for personality.

Hi, I'm Jennifer, a junior studying Electrical Engineering and Computer Science at UC Berkeley. I'm passionate about chip design and optimization, especially in ASIC design and the ways AI can be used to advance computer architecture.

I'm currently an undergraduate researcher with Prof. Karandikar's group, using agent loops to optimize RTL across the full RTL-to-GDSII tapeout flow and integrating Cadence Jasper formal verification into it. I'm also on course staff for EECS 151 (Digital Design & Integrated Circuits), and a machine learning intern at BFAI Semiconductor, building computer vision pipelines for semiconductor defect detection.

3-Stage Pipelined RISC-V CPU (EECS 151)

2026

A 3-stage pipelined RISC-V CPU in SystemVerilog with hazard detection, forwarding, stalls, and branch/jump flushing. Placed 1st on the class leaderboard by frequency, with a top-10 figure of merit.

  • SystemVerilog
  • Synopsys VCS/Verdi
  • Cadence Genus/Innovus/Joules

Verified with assembly tests and post-synthesis simulation, then taken through synthesis, place-and-route, and power analysis. The view above is the routed layout.

Semiconductor SEM Defect Classification (Carinthia)

2026

Benchmarked four vision models (Swin, Xception, TinyViT, EfficientFormer) for SEM defect classification in one shared PyTorch/timm multi-seed pipeline.

  • Python
  • PyTorch / timm
  • Computer Vision

The best model reached 0.99 macro-F1, and imbalance-aware training lifted recall on the rarest class (55 images, ~73× long-tail) to 100%.
